Foundational Research
User & Non-User Perceptions of AI
Explored how users and non-users perceive generative AI tools to inform TikTok's AI communications and product marketing strategy.
Background

As TikTok continues to roll out AI-powered features across the platform, understanding how users perceive generative AI tools is critical. These perceptions shape user receptivity and directly inform the messaging strategy when introducing and discussing AI tools. This foundational qualitative study explored perceptions of AI in general and TikTok's use of AI specifically, generating early learnings to guide communications, media outreach, and product marketing strategies.

Research Objectives
  • Understand users' awareness, attitudes, and mental models of AI
  • Explore perceptions of TikTok's use of AI — including perceived benefits, concerns, and impact on platform experience
Methodology

1:1 semi-structured interviews with both non-users and users across varying levels of AI usage and awareness.

Semi-Structured Interviews Qualitative Users & Non-Users
Key Findings
Users are aware that AI tools are rapidly expanding on social media platforms.
Users view AI as a helpful tool that can aid with planning, synthesizing, and learning new things.
Trust in AI hinges on accuracy and the ability to help complete intended tasks.
Users are concerned that over-relying on AI will impact critical thinking skills and interrupt online experiences.
The dominant mental model: AI is a helpful coworker that can make mistakes.
TikTok users expect the platform to roll out AI features aligned with their in-app behaviors.
Recommendations
01
Position TikTok's AI tools as assistants that handle the "heavy lifting" of searching for information, helping users locate content at a quicker rate.
02
Emphasize utility over underlying technology. Messaging should prioritize simple, jargon-free language that focuses on how the tool assists the user.
03
Promote AI tools as experience enhancers rather than interrupters — tools that work with how users already like to use the app.
Quantitative Research
Quarterly User Trust Benchmark
Developed trackable measures of user trust across eight global markets to inform cross-functional trust-building efforts at TikTok.
Background

The quarterly User Trust Benchmark aims to create robust and trackable measures of user trust that relate to sustained trust-building efforts by cross-functional teams spanning TikTok. User survey data was collected from adult users representative of TikTok's core user cohorts across eight key global markets (US, UK, DE, FR, ID, JP, MX, and BR). The benchmark measures both overall user trust of TikTok and the extent to which users agree with 16 attitudinal statements that map onto cross-functional trust efforts across the platform.

Research Objectives
  • Understand how user trust varies by region
  • Create robust, trackable measures of user trust by region and activity level
Methodology

Quantitative survey with adult users (18+) across eight global markets. Participants rated their overall trust of TikTok on a 1–10 scale and responded to 16 attitudinal statements (e.g., "TikTok respects my data privacy," "TikTok is a place to learn") using a Likert-scale format.

Survey Quantitative 8 Global Markets Longitudinal
Key Findings
Trust scores varied meaningfully across regions, with markets in Southeast Asia and Latin America indexing higher than Western European markets.
Higher-activity users reported stronger trust across most attitudinal dimensions, suggesting engagement and trust are mutually reinforcing.
Data privacy was the lowest-scoring attitudinal dimension globally, representing a consistent gap across all eight markets.
Learning and entertainment-related attitudinal items ("TikTok is a place to learn") scored highest, signaling these as established trust anchors.
Regional trust profiles diverged on safety and content quality dimensions, pointing to the need for market-specific communications strategies.
Tracking across quarters revealed that trust is sensitive to platform events, underscoring the value of repeated measurement over time.
Recommendations
01
Prioritize data privacy messaging in trust-building campaigns globally. Given this dimension's consistently low scores, communications that clearly explain how TikTok handles user data can close the largest trust gap.
02
Tailor regional strategies to local trust profiles. Western European markets require distinct approaches — especially around safety and transparency — compared to high-trust markets in Southeast Asia and Latin America.
03
Leverage learning and entertainment as trust entry points. These dimensions are already established strengths and can serve as a foundation for messaging that bridges into lower-scoring areas like privacy and safety.
Qualitative Research
User Perceptions of Data Privacy & Handling
A follow-up qualitative study exploring why low-trust users distrust TikTok's data practices — and what would shift their perceptions.
Background

Data privacy emerged as the lowest-scoring attitudinal dimension in the quarterly User Trust Benchmark — a consistent gap across all eight global markets. To understand the drivers behind this finding, a follow-up qualitative study was conducted with US-based users who disagreed that TikTok respects their data privacy and handles data appropriately as a company. By recruiting specifically from the low end of the trust spectrum, this study was designed to surface the underlying beliefs, mental models, and experiences shaping data privacy concerns.

Research Objectives
  • Understand what drives distrust in TikTok's data privacy and handling practices
  • Identify the mental models users hold around how TikTok collects and uses their data
  • Surface what information or experiences could shift user perceptions
Methodology

1:1 semi-structured interviews with US adults who scored low on trust and disagreed with data privacy attitudinal statements in the benchmark. Participants were recruited across varying levels of overall platform trust to capture a range of skepticism — from general wariness to active concern.

Semi-Structured Interviews Qualitative Low-Trust Users United States
Key Findings
Trust is easily lost and slowly earned. Users tend to start with moderate trust that deepens through sustained positive experiences — but a single negative incident can rapidly unravel that confidence.
Bad actors are a primary trust disruptor. Low-trust users frequently pointed to encounters with fake profiles and scams as the root of their skepticism, extrapolating isolated incidents into a broader distrust of the platform.
Users want control but don't actively seek it. Participants expressed a strong desire to feel informed and in control of their data, yet few had sought out privacy settings or resources on their own.
Secondhand narratives carry significant weight. Media coverage shaped perceptions as much as direct experience — users who hadn't encountered problems themselves remained skeptical due to external narratives around TikTok.
TikTok feels impersonal compared to competitors. Users perceived TikTok as faceless relative to platforms associated with recognizable leadership figures, contributing to a sense of distance and reduced accountability.
The geopolitical context amplifies US-specific distrust. Concerns about foreign ownership and government data access were prominent among US participants, adding a layer of skepticism that extended beyond the platform's actual practices.
Recommendations
01
Respond to trust incidents proactively and transparently. When scams or bad actors surface, communicating what TikTok is doing — and keeping users informed throughout — can move low-trust users toward moderate trust.
02
Bring existing privacy initiatives to where users already are. Efforts like Project Texas and Oracle data storage partnerships are largely unknown to users. Amplifying these through creators, in-feed content, and marketing can close the awareness gap.
03
Humanize the brand through visible leadership. Associating a recognizable spokesperson with TikTok's trust and safety efforts can address the sense of facelessness that contributes to distrust.
04
Counter the negative media narrative with a US-specific transparency strategy. Given the geopolitical climate, US users need direct, factual communications about data storage and government access — not just general privacy messaging.
